## Fix these before you submit (in order)

### 1. The nine-year gap is the thing that kills this application
Emory bachelor's degrees in **May 2018**, Notre Dame expected graduation
**May 2028**, one 2021 summer internship in between, and nothing else. A screener
reads that in four seconds and has no story for it.

Your strategy notes (`career/stories.md:56-58`) say to drop advanced degrees and
faculty roles "because they suggest you're not an undergrad." That is the wrong
instinct — it does not remove the gap, it removes the *explanation* for the gap
and leaves the gap. Whatever you were actually doing from 2018 to 2025, put it on
the resume, even in one compressed line, and give the cover letter one honest
sentence about why you are pursuing business analytics now. A deliberate
career-changer is a fine story. An unexplained decade is not.

### 2. One project, fully specified
The single highest-leverage change per the hiring manager: *one* Paychex project
told end to end — what it was named or about, how big the data was, what **you**
personally built, and what decision or number came out of it. Right now every
professional bullet is a category label ("analytical models," "trends,"
"actionable insights"), not an accomplishment. The hiring manager said this one
fix alone would move the file from PASS to INTERVIEW.

**Do not use the numbers in your strategy doc.** `career/stories.md:102` and
`:161` contain "5,000+ customer accounts," "increased adoption by 15%," "saved Z
time / improved efficiency by N%." Those are fill-in-the-blank templates you wrote
for yourself, not facts from the job. The submitter agent refused to use them and
so should you — in an interview those become a credibility problem. Use only what
you can actually recall and defend.

### 3. Fix the impossible date
`career/profile.md:43` — "Notre Dame Business Analytics Club | 2027 – Present."
The deadline is September 2026. As written it reads as carelessness, and it
destroys your only case-competition evidence.

### 4. Get one practice-area tie
Healthcare, education, or commercial operations scored **0 — no evidence** with
the hiring manager, while your draft cover letter (`career/voice.md:16`) says you
"have studied your work across healthcare, education, and operations
transformation." That claim is currently unbacked. Either back it (a course, a
case competition, a club project, an article you can actually discuss) or drop
the sentence. An unbacked claim invites exactly the question you can't answer.

### 5. Address Chicago
Also scored 0. One line confirming you can be on-site three days a week in
Chicago removes a free reason to cut you.
