## Step 3: The three strongest stories

### Story 1 — The Paychex cross-functional analysis (career/profile.md:19-23)
The one piece of real professional evidence, and it maps onto three separate Huron
requirements at once: building models, working across Product/Engineering/Finance, and
presenting to people who do not read code. **Why it matters to Huron:** Huron staffs interns
directly onto engagement teams, where the daily job is exactly this triad — analyze, align
several functions, then say the "so what" out loud. Junyuan has already done the sequence end
to end in a real company, which is more than most rising juniors can claim.

Tell it at the level the file supports: payroll, compliance and HR-operations datasets;
Python models; findings carried to stakeholders in several departments. Do **not** attach the
"5,000+ accounts," "three user segments," or "increased adoption by 15%" figures from
career/stories.md:161 — those are written there as an illustrative template, not as record.

### Story 2 — Emory Data Science Club, VP of Development (career/profile.md:38-41)
Ran a program, not just attended one: 15+ events, 100+ students a year, speakers recruited
from industry. **Why it matters to Huron:** recruiting outside speakers is a small-scale
version of the client-facing ask — persuading a busy stranger that your agenda is worth their
time — and the event count is a concrete, verifiable number in a resume that is otherwise thin
on metrics. The claim to make is "organized 15+ events serving 100+ students." The claim in
the draft cover letter, "grew engagement across diverse student backgrounds"
(career/voice.md:18), goes past what line 39 ("Led initiatives to grow club membership and
engagement") actually documents, and I'd cut it.

### Story 3 — The CS + Economics/Math foundation applied to a business problem (career/profile.md:12, 9)
Three quantitative disciplines, now pointed deliberately at business analytics coursework at
Notre Dame. **Why it matters to Huron:** the posting's own language is "interns join us from a
wide range of backgrounds," and Huron's digital and operations practices need people who can
sit between a data team and a client executive. This is a background story, not an
achievement story, so it should be the shortest of the three.
