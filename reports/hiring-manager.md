## 3. Unexplained items on the face of the profile

These are not scored, but they shape my read:

- **A ten-year gap.** Emory B.S./B.A. completed May 2018; Notre Dame graduation May 2028. The profile accounts for 2018–2028 with exactly one three-month internship in 2021. Roughly nine years are blank. I do not assume anything bad, but I cannot screen around a gap this size, and a candidate who knows it is there and does not address it is making my job harder.
- **The only work experience is five years old and three months long.** Every single professional claim in this profile rests on Paychex, summer 2021.
- **A date that cannot be right.** "Notre Dame Business Analytics Club | 2027 – Present" — the application deadline is September 2026. Either the date is wrong or the membership has not started. On a one-page document, a hiring manager notices.
- **Seniority mismatch.** A 2018 CS/Econ/Math graduate applying to an undergraduate summer internship at ~$30/hour is unusual. Huron says interns come from a wide range of backgrounds and I mean that, but the profile gives me no narrative for this, and I will not invent one.

## 4. Three first-interview questions

1. **"Walk me through one Paychex project end to end — what the question was, who asked it, what the data actually looked like, what you built, and what the business did differently afterward."** Everything in the experience section is a category label. This question is un-fakeable by anyone who did not do the work: I want the messy specifics — where the data was wrong, what you had to throw out, what the answer turned out to be.

2. **"Take me through your timeline from graduating Emory in 2018 to enrolling at Notre Dame."** Directly on the gap. I am listening for a straight, unembarrassed account — and, if there was work in that period, why none of it is on the profile.

3. **"Describe a time in the Business Analytics Club — a case competition or a consulting simulation — where your team disagreed on the recommendation. What was your position, and how did it resolve?"** The club bullets are pure participation language ("active participant," "engaged in"). This tests whether there is any real, recent, collaborative work behind them, and it is the closest proxy I have for client-team behavior.

---

## 5. Biggest reason to pass

**Every professional claim is a category, not an accomplishment, and all of them rest on one three-month internship from five years ago.** "Analytical models," "trends," "actionable business insights," "multiple departments" — I cannot tell from this document whether the candidate ran a forecasting project that changed a staffing decision or cleaned spreadsheets for a manager. With 200 applicants, many of whom will hand me a named project with a number attached, I do not have the interview slots to find out. The unexplained nine-year gap compounds it: the profile does not give me enough surface area to justify one of eight seats.

---

## 6. Decision

**PASS** — a near-miss, not a rejection on quality. The technical foundation is genuinely strong (Python/R/SQL, predictive modeling, a CS/Econ/Math degree), the cross-functional bullet is real, and the graduation year fits. This is a candidate I would want to be wrong about.

**What would change my mind:**

1. **One quantified project, fully specified.** Name it, size the data, state what you personally built, and state the outcome in a number or a decision. One such bullet moves this from PASS to INTERVIEW on its own, and it is the highest-leverage change available.
2. **Address the gap in the cover letter or a profile line.** One honest sentence. Silence reads worse than whatever the answer is.
3. **Any tie to healthcare, education, or commercial operations** — a course, a case competition, a project, a volunteer role. A stated reason for wanting Huron specifically, rather than consulting generally.
4. **Recent, substantive evidence.** A 2026 case competition placement or a current analytics project would show the candidate is operating now, not recalling 2021.
5. **Fix the club date and confirm Chicago availability.** Small, but the date error costs credibility on a document whose main problem is already that I have to take it on faith.
